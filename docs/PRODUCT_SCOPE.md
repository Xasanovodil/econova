# Econova product direction

Econova is a shared economics game for Uzbek school students, beginning with one school. The primary experience is a navigable 3D city on phones, with Telegram Mini App integration planned. Players create, operate, jointly own, trade with and acquire businesses. Decisions should change visible queues, work, deliveries and buildings, with financial reports that explain why.

The first working slice is a two-partner auto repair business: 6m/4m founding contributions, 60/40 ownership, employees and parts, a broken-lift capacity decision, completed work and payroll, and a 12m acquisition by a third participant with 7.2m/4.8m payouts. The company retains its assets, obligations and history when ownership changes.

The long-term catalog contains 35 industries and 36 distinct building designs. Each industry needs its own operating constraints; industries should be exposed as playable only once their operations work. The city should grow with unique verified participants, and resident demand should reflect diverse budgets, preferences, trust and location rather than one national stereotype.

Build order:

- **P0:** one playable 3D street, shared persistent state, joint founding, repair-shop operations and acquisition.
- **P1:** four additional core industries, resident demand, bank lending, expansion, school calendar, manager mode and learning laboratory.
- **P2:** verified joining, migration and district growth, seven additional industries, B2B supply chains and logistics.
- **P3:** the remaining industry/building catalog and longer progression paths.
- **P4:** additional schools, intercity trade and broader competitions after pilot evidence.

The server owns money, stock, contracts, ownership and time. AI may explain verified facts but must not invent transactions. Normal gameplay should remain usable without an LLM. The simulation population is separate from the number of people rendered on screen. Residents and companies must not create duplicate sales, money or capacity.

Natural Uzbek, accessible actions, clear prerequisites, understandable feedback, mobile controls and multiple measures of progress remain central. Grants and virtual credit must not be presented as profit. Shared simulations need real authentication, transactional storage, retry protection and scoped views. Local demonstrations and future features should be clearly identified.

The detailed private handoff supplied in chat remains available in the task workspace. This summary records the implementation direction without embedding links to personal conversation files.
